The Grain of Wheat Must Die

March 24, 2024 • Fifth Sunday of Lent Fr. John Mukasa 8 min read

Readings: Jeremiah 31:31-34; Hebrews 5:7-9; John 12:20-33

"Unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it produces much fruit."

My dear brothers and sisters in Christ,

These words of Jesus, spoken just days before His passion, contain a profound mystery of our faith: life comes through death. It seems counterintuitive in a world that tells us to cling to life, to protect ourselves, to avoid suffering at all costs. Yet Jesus presents us with the image of the grain of wheat — something so small, so seemingly insignificant, that holds within it the potential for abundant life.

Think about what happens to a seed. When you plant it, it must be buried in the dark soil. It must break open. It must, in a sense, die to its existence as a single seed so that it can become something more — a stalk of wheat that produces many grains. If it remained safely in the barn, it would never fulfill its purpose.

We are like that seed. God has placed within each of us potential for great fruitfulness. But too often, we remain as single seeds — self-protective, afraid to take risks, unwilling to let go of our comfort zones. We hold onto our time, our talents, our resources as if they belong to us alone. And in doing so, we remain just a single grain.

So what must "die" in us? What must we let go of so that we can bear fruit?

Perhaps it's our pride that needs to die — the need to always be right, to have the last word, to protect our image. Perhaps it's our selfishness — the tendency to put our own needs before others, to hoard rather than to share. Perhaps it's our fear — fear of what others might think, fear of stepping out in faith, fear of loving too much because we might get hurt.

When these things die in us, space is created for something new to grow. Humility takes root, and we become more approachable. Generosity flourishes, and we become channels of God's blessings. Courage awakens, and we become instruments of God's love in places we never imagined.

Jesus Himself is the ultimate example of this truth. He was the grain of wheat who fell to the ground and died — and from His death has come an abundant harvest of salvation for all humanity. As He says in today's Gospel, "When I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw everyone to myself." His death was not an end but a beginning, not a failure but the greatest victory in history.

In the second reading, the Letter to the Hebrews tells us that Jesus "learned obedience from what he suffered." Even the Son of God embraced the path of suffering and death, trusting that the Father would raise Him up. This is the pattern of Christian life: through death to life, through surrender to victory, through the cross to the resurrection.

As we draw closer to Holy Week, let us ask ourselves: What is God calling me to let die in my life? What seeds of selfishness, fear, or pride need to be buried so that I can bear fruit for His kingdom? And what new life might God be wanting to bring forth in me?

The grain of wheat must die — but oh, the fruit it produces! May we have the courage to let go, to trust, and to believe that in losing our life we will find it.

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.
